  • August, live: only 8.6% of sessions ever add something to cart (220 of 2,567). Of those, 171 reach checkout (77.7%), and 41 of those complete (24.0%). So 2,347 sessions are lost before the cart, 49 between cart and checkout, and 130 at payment.
  • The 20-Aug payment-step failure did NOT become the new baseline. On 20-Aug, 10 of 14 cart-adders reached checkout and ZERO paid. Across the three days that followed — 21, 22 and 23-Aug — 21 sessions reached checkout and 6 completed (28.6%, better than the month’s 24.0%), and each day booked exactly 2 orders. 23-Aug ran the tightest cart→checkout step of the month (11 of 12 cart-adders, 91.7%) and converted 2 of them. Treat 18 and 20 August as isolated payment-step incidents worth checking in the provider decline logs, not as an ongoing outage.
  • 24-Aug failed for the opposite reason. It took 60 sessions — the fourth-lowest traffic day of August, behind 6-Aug (38), 15-Aug (46) and 9-Aug (47) — and only 2 of them (3.3%) added to cart, the weakest cart-add rate since 18-Aug’s 4.1%. Nothing reached the payment step to fail. That is a thin-demand Monday, and the fix for it is upstream of checkout entirely.

Why is repeat purchase so low (14.3% of orders, 15.5% of sales)?

This isn't a one-off dip — it's structural. Every acquisition cohort in the past year shows the same pattern.

  • Average month-1 repurchase rate across the 11 cohorts with enough history is just 0.5% — it was this low in Jul 2025 and it's still this low in Mar 2026.
  • No lifecycle/win-back signal shows up in the data: cohorts that grew fastest (Jan–Mar 2026, 121–149 customers each) retain no better than the small early cohorts.
  • New customers alone drove Rs 40,000 of this period's Rs 47,332 in sales — the business is currently entirely dependent on new-customer acquisition.

Why can't marketing ROI be confirmed?

88% of orders trace back to paid/social channels, but no spend or cost data was included in this export — ROAS and CPA cannot be calculated from what's available.

  • Instagram carries the most traffic and the most attributed sales (Rs 40,903 across the trailing 31 days) but its conversion rate (1.82%) trails Facebook's (2.74%) — without cost data, it's impossible to say which channel is actually more profitable to fund.
  • Direct traffic (549 sessions, the third-largest source) converts at just 0.36%, the worst of any channel with meaningful volume — worth checking whether this is bookmarked/repeat browsing or a broken attribution tag.
  • Ask your ads platforms (Meta Ads Manager, Google Ads) directly for spend by campaign — that's the one number this export is missing.

How the August weeks are tracking

Real Monday–Sunday ISO calendar weeks, not 7-day buckets counted from the 1st. Week 1 only ever covers the 1–2 Aug tail of the ISO week that began Mon 27-Jul (its 27–31 Jul portion is frozen in the July workbook), and Week 5 has only just opened with a single day — neither is comparable to a full week. Weeks 2, 3 and 4 are all complete and directly comparable.

Week 4 closed 32% below Week 3 on almost the same traffic — the gap is conversion, not reach

  • Week 4 (Mon 17–Sun 23 Aug) is now COMPLETE at 12 orders and Rs 15,808 total sales on 756 sessions. Week 3 did 18 orders and Rs 23,318 on 799 sessions. Sales fell 32.2% while traffic fell only 5.4%: Week 4 converted 1.59% of sessions against Week 3’s 2.13%, and completed 21.43% of the checkouts it reached against Week 3’s 25.00%.
  • Week 3 was carried by two days: 13-Aug and 14-Aug booked 6 orders each and together still account for 28.6% of August orders and 32.8% of August gross. Week 4 had no equivalent peak — its best day was 19-Aug at 4 orders, and it carried two zero-order days (18 and 20 Aug). Week 3 was concentrated; Week 4 was flat and low.
  • Week 5 (Mon 24–Sun 30 Aug) has opened with 24-Aug alone: 0 orders, Rs 0, 60 sessions. It is marked PARTIAL at 1 of 7 days and must not be read as a decline until it closes on 30-Aug.
  • Returning-customer share still swings with no trend (0% / 18.2% / 35.3% / 0% / 0% across Weeks 1–5). Not one of the 6 customers who bought between 21 and 24 Aug was a repeat buyer, which pulls the month’s returning share down from 22.9% to 19.5%. The Week 3 figure remains the highest recorded in either month, but on only 17 customers.
  • August has now had seven zero-order days (2, 6, 12, 15, 18, 20 and 24-Aug). Five of them — 2, 6, 12, 15 and 24-Aug — combined low sessions with low cart additions, i.e. interest failures. Only 18-Aug and 20-Aug were high-traffic days that failed inside the funnel, and neither pattern repeated in the four days added since.
