Why is site-wide conversion only 1.3%?

3,023 sessions produced just 40 completed checkouts. The drop happens early — before checkout is even a factor.

  • Only 5.5% of all sessions ever add something to cart (165 of 3,023) — once a shopper reaches checkout, they finish 33.3% of the time, which is a healthy rate.
  • Blog Article pages bring in 306 sessions (10% of all traffic) and generated zero cart adds — that traffic is informational, not shopping intent, and it drags the blended conversion rate down.
  • Product pages carry 77% of sessions but only convert 6.4% of visits to a cart add — the single largest lever for improving overall conversion.
  • Instagram is the largest paid/social channel (1,809 sessions) but converts at 1.82%, well below Facebook's 2.74% on far less traffic (328 sessions) — the biggest channel is not the most efficient one.

Why is repeat purchase so low (14.3% of orders, 15.5% of sales)?

This isn't a one-off dip — it's structural. Every acquisition cohort in the past year shows the same pattern.

  • Average month-1 repurchase rate across the 11 cohorts with enough history is just 0.5% — it was this low in Jul 2025 and it's still this low in Mar 2026.
  • No lifecycle/win-back signal shows up in the data: cohorts that grew fastest (Jan–Mar 2026, 121–149 customers each) retain no better than the small early cohorts.
  • New customers alone drove Rs 40,000 of this period's Rs 47,332 in sales — the business is currently entirely dependent on new-customer acquisition.

Why can't marketing ROI be confirmed?

88% of orders trace back to paid/social channels, but no spend or cost data was included in this export — ROAS and CPA cannot be calculated from what's available.

  • Instagram carries the most traffic and the most attributed sales (Rs 40,903 across the trailing 31 days) but its conversion rate (1.82%) trails Facebook's (2.74%) — without cost data, it's impossible to say which channel is actually more profitable to fund.
  • Direct traffic (549 sessions, the third-largest source) converts at just 0.36%, the worst of any channel with meaningful volume — worth checking whether this is bookmarked/repeat browsing or a broken attribution tag.
  • Ask your ads platforms (Meta Ads Manager, Google Ads) directly for spend by campaign — that's the one number this export is missing.

What happened in week 4?

Flagging this as a trend worth investigating internally — the data shows the decline clearly but not the cause. (Week boundaries corrected to real Monday–Sunday calendar weeks on 28-Jul-2026; previously this section referenced an incorrectly-bucketed "Week 3".)

Week 4 (20–26 Jul) is the weakest full week on conversion, despite having the most traffic

  • Conversion fell to 1.03%, down steadily from 1.51% in Week 1, 1.37% in Week 2, and 1.19% in Week 3 — a consistent week-over-week decline.
  • Week 4 had the most sessions of any week (872) yet converted the worst — traffic is growing but converting less each week.
  • Returning-customer share swings widely with no clear trend (0% / 15.4% / 30% / 11.1% across Weeks 1–4) rather than steadily improving, consistent with the structural new-customer dependency flagged above.
  • This lines up with no return-rate spike or stock-out in the underlying data, so the cause is more likely external (a paused campaign, a pricing/promo change, or a site issue) than a product problem — worth checking against your own calendar for that week.
